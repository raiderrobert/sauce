## Product visuals

- **Marketing:** Show the real product (actual UI, lightly edited screenshots, even simple skewed product cards) instead of stock images or generic feature icons; don't bury what the product is in small text. Unrelated stock throws the page off; graphics elevate a page more than anything else. ([jain/captivating-sections 1:23], [jain/landing-page-levels 0:44], [jain/saas-vibe-code-mistakes 5:48])
- **Marketing:** Use imagery that visibly relates to the audience's world, and show images of products or options so visitors can tell unfamiliar names apart. Builds trust and explains options without reading, like a picture menu. ([jain/slept-on-trends 7:51], [jain/vibe-coding-cant-fix 0:58])
- **Marketing:** Crop and zoom screenshots to the important parts, or better, craft visuals that show exactly what the product does. Curates what visitors see. ([jain/landing-page-levels 4:59])
- **Marketing:** Pick images for the layout's aspect ratio and expect heavy cropping; for cutout-text layouts, keep nothing important in the corner that gets cut off. ([jain/website-layouts 2:13])

## Hero sections

- **Marketing:** Write one actionable hero headline (~7 words) promising exactly what the product does, with ~14-word subtext that directly supports it. Communicates the message as fast as possible. ([jain/landing-page-levels 3:04], [jain/learn-ui-in-10-minutes 1:04], [jain/vibe-coding-cant-fix 1:23])
- **Marketing:** Choose from the standard hero layouts (text left/image right, text over a full-screen image, text above an image); a stacked hero with big text above a big product image and plenty of space reads most modern as of 2026. Don't let the text block dominate. Only a handful of layouts exist; the stacked hero lets the page breathe and invites scrolling. ([jain/landing-page-levels 2:28], [jain/learn-ui-in-10-minutes 0:41], [jain/modern-layouts 2:37])
- **Marketing:** Differentiate the standard hero with a distinctive display font, an inline diagram, a live or animated product image (e.g. an AI thinking state), or a 3D focal point. Everyone uses the same layout; a live product moment is eye-catching. ([jain/google-ai-redesign 1:57], [jain/modern-layouts 2:45], [jain/no-code-3d-website 1:04])
- **Marketing:** Pair the hero headline with subtext plus one primary and one secondary CTA, and give the nav CTA and hero CTA the same label. Matching labels build the right mental model that both go to the same place. ([jain/landing-page-levels 3:28], [jain/skincare-redesign 5:08])
- **Marketing:** Consider a type-in prompt or search bar as the hero CTA instead of a plain button. A user already engaged in an action is more likely to finish it. ([jain/google-ai-redesign 1:08])
- **Marketing:** Keep contextual illustrations around a centered core message rather than stripping them for clarity. They explain the product without reading; without them the hero feels empty. ([jain/boring-design-hacks 0:27])
- **Marketing:** Arrange 'scattered' hero images deliberately, then check: larger elements sit lower, smaller ones on top, and none enters a safe margin around the text. The scatter reads as random only when the size order and the text margin hold. ([jain/vibe-coding-cant-fix 5:54])

## Copy

- **Marketing:** Cut vague, long feature copy drastically (e.g. by 90%) to short lines and let bigger visuals carry the story. No one stops to read a block of text. ([jain/landing-page-levels 1:07], [jain/vibe-coding-cant-fix 3:49])
- **Marketing:** Don't spend copy on what's already visible: no company name beside the logo, no labels like 'Financial dashboard' over a dashboard. It's redundant. ([jain/finance-dashboard-redesign 1:09], [jain/landing-page-levels 1:17])
- **Marketing:** Write feature copy about how the product helps, not just what it does, in friendly, natural language rather than corporate jargon (e.g. 'we sweat the details'). Benefit-focused, human copy is more persuasive. ([jain/boring-design-hacks 5:34], [jain/landing-page-levels 7:54])
- **Marketing:** Paraphrase long specs and link to a full specs page rather than hiding them in accordions. People skip accordions and prefer pictures. ([jain/tesla-wannabe-redesign 5:07])

## Page structure and footers

- **Marketing:** Break text-heavy runs with a brand moment: a thin cropped photo or a wide landscape shot with an overlay, carrying one short statement set large and centered with lots of space. Acts as a spacer and creates impact. ([jain/awful-to-amazing 2:38], [jain/learn-ui-in-10-minutes 4:11], [jain/website-layouts 1:31])
- **Marketing:** Build a logical top-to-bottom flow and keep the page no longer than needed: order sections so each answers the visitor's next question (e.g. what it is, how it works, proof, pricing, final CTA), and cut any section that repeats content already shown. Abrupt sections feel disconnected; good flow encourages scrolling. *Note: The section order is an editorial example.* ([jain/landing-page-levels 3:49], [jain/tesla-wannabe-redesign 1:10])
- **Marketing:** Turn a static row of three cards into a clickable tabbed or multi-select section. Adds interactivity and variety and makes sections more compact. ([jain/landing-page-levels 5:23])
- **Marketing:** Use stacked cards that slide up over each other to present 3-5 options in detail. ([jain/website-layouts 6:21])
- **Marketing:** Size the footer to the links: with about five or fewer, center them with the logo above, socials below, copyright left and credits right; switch to a multi-column footer only beyond that. Giant multi-column footers look awful with few links. ([jain/crucial-ui-elements 7:57])

## Social proof and trust

- **Marketing:** If the product has well-recognized customers, show a row of their logos: put only the most important in one row, or use a slow marquee whose edges fade out with a gradient and progressive blur. 41 of 50 software sites had one; visitors read logos as important companies using the product. ([jain/landing-page-levels 5:54], [jain/modern-layouts 0:38])
- **Marketing:** If the product has testimonials, badges or a dedicated proof page, add them as social proof beyond the logo row. Strengthens the brand. ([jain/landing-page-levels 5:54], [jain/vibe-coding-cant-fix 3:57])
- **Marketing:** Include a privacy and security section for any product that handles user data. Users need reassurance. ([jain/google-ai-redesign 5:32])
