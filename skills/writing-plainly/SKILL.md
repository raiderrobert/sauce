# Writing Plainly

Use the user's approved examples as the standard. Plain writing should sound like them, not like a generic house style.

## Examples

Read these pairs before drafting or editing. The preferred versions show the intended voice; the counterexamples are not templates.

- [PR description](examples/pr-description.md): the original and the user's rewrite.
- [README opening](examples/readme-opening.md): a generated opening and the existing version the user prefers.

Explain what the reader needs to know. Keep useful requirements and precise names. Leave out an inventory of the work unless the reader needs it.

The examples are not length limits or bans on headings, lists, or verification details. Keep required sections and information the reader needs to act safely or understand a limitation. Match the amount of detail to the task rather than forcing every document into two paragraphs.

Return the requested writing. Explain the edits when asked, rather than attaching a style review to every draft.

## Adding examples

Keep each counterexample and user-approved version together, with a short note about the difference. Preserve the approved wording rather than silently polishing it. Identify generated text as generated, not as a historical original. Label assistant suggestions as suggestions until the user approves them.

Add examples as the user supplies or approves them. A few examples do not establish their voice for every kind of writing.
